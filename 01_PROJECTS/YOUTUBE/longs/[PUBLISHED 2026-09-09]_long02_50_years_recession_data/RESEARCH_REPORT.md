## 3. The 4 Counterintuitive Revelations (The Data Core)

Our 50-year empirical dataset (1970–2025 from NBER and Federal Reserve Economic Data) reveals 4 truths that contradict common intuition:

### 1. The Stock Market is NOT the Economy (The Lead/Lag Paradox)
- The stock market is a **leading indicator**, while recessions are declared in **hindsight**.
- On average, the S&P 500 peaks **6 to 9 months BEFORE** the recession officially begins.
- By the time the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER) officially announces a recession (which takes an average of 7 months due to backward-looking GDP revisions), the market has usually already digested the worst news.
- **The Shocking Stat**: In almost every recession since 1970, the stock market **bottoms and begins a violent new bull market 3 to 5 months BEFORE the recession officially ends**, right when economic headlines are at their absolute bleakest.

### 2. The Asymmetry of Pain vs Growth (10 Months vs 5–10 Years)
- Since 1950, the average U.S. recession has lasted **only 10.4 months**.
- In contrast, the average economic expansion lasts **64 months (over 5 years)**, with recent expansions lasting 10+ years (2009–2020).
- The visual insight: Recessions are sharp, brief compressions in a multi-decade upward trajectory.

### 3. The Jobless Recovery (The Human Asymmetry)
- While corporate profits and equity markets recover rapidly, the labor market suffers long-tail scars.
- Unemployment spikes abruptly (the elevator down), but recovering to pre-recession employment levels takes an average of **3 to 5 years** (the stairs up).
- This explains why everyday people feel like a recession lasts for years even after GDP turns positive.

### 4. The Signal vs Noise of Recession Indicators
- **Yield Curve Inversion (10-Year minus 2-Year Treasury)**: Accurately preceded every recession since 1970, BUT the lead time varies wildly from 8 to 24 months. Trying to trade the exact timing based on the yield curve has historically resulted in missing massive late-cycle market gains.
- **The Sahm Rule**: Triggers when the 3-month moving average of unemployment rises by 0.50% above its 12-month low. A remarkably clean real-time indicator.

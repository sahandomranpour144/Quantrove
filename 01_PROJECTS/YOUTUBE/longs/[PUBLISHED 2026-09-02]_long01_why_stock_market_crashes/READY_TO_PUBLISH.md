### Description
```
In just 22 trading days in 2020, the stock market erased more than a third of its total value. Whenever a financial crash happens, the media quickly attributes the collapse to "investor panic" — but panic is merely a symptom, not the root cause. Why does the panic start in the first place, and why do markets behave in such extreme, violent ways across completely different historical eras?

To understand the true anatomy of a market crash, we analyze three of the largest economic downturns in modern history: the 1929 Wall Street Crash (driven by 10:1 margin debt and a multi-year banking collapse), the 2008 Global Financial Crisis (triggered by opaque subprime mortgage derivatives and Lehman Brothers' $639B insolvency), and the 2020 COVID shock (the fastest bear market plunge and recovery ever recorded).

While every crash begins with a unique real-world trigger, they all share an identical underlying mechanism: confidence ruptures, market liquidity evaporates, and millions of investors rush for a single exit door at the same time. Understand how this liquidity stampede works, how to navigate sudden volatility, and subscribe for part two where we explore 50 years of post-crash economic recovery data.
```

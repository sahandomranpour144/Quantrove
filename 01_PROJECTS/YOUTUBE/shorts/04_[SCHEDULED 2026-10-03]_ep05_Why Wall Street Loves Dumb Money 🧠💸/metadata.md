Why do multi-billion dollar algorithmic trading firms pay retail brokers to trade against complete beginners?

Most people assume high-frequency traders want to front-run retail investors or steal their stock picks. Others think market makers make fortunes taking massive directional bets.

The mathematical reality is adverse selection. 

When a market maker trades against an institutional fund, that fund has faster data and private research. That order carries severe information risk.

Retail flow is completely uninformed. It is small, random, and harmless — allowing market makers to capture the spread with virtually zero inventory risk.
